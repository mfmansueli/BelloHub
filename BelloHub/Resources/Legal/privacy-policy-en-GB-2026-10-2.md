# Privacy policy

Effective {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}. This policy covers the Bello Hub app and the booking pages at {{WEB_BASE_URL}}. Part A is the common part, the same in every European language. Part B is the national annex for the United Kingdom, with notes for Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa.

# Part A. Common part

## A1. Who we are
Bello Hub is operated by {{OPERATOR_NAME}}, {{OPERATOR_ADDRESS}}, contact {{OPERATOR_EMAIL}}. We have two roles. For the professional's own account we are the controller. For the bookings and client information a professional keeps with Bello Hub, **the professional is the controller and we are her processor**, acting on her instructions under the data processing agreement in our terms.

## A2. What we collect
- **The professional:** her Apple sign-in identifier and the email Apple shares (it may be a private relay address) or, if she signs in with her phone, her phone number; her name and business name; the public profile she chooses to publish; her plan and subscription status; push notification tokens; language, region and time zone.
- **A client who books online:** name, phone number, email address (used to send a verification code and the confirmation), the service, add-ons and number of guests, the time, any short note she writes, the status of the booking, waiting-list requests, requests to move or cancel, a "running late" notice, and her language.
- **What a client sends sealed:** a health form, a check-in after the visit (how she feels and an optional photo) and requests about her information. They are encrypted in her browser and we cannot read them (A4).
- **To keep the free allowance honest:** a count of client registrations on the account and, in the professional's own iCloud, a list of anonymous client identifiers and dates (no names or contact details).
- **Nothing else.** No advertising identifiers, no analytics, no tracking cookies, no crash reporter that reads text. The booking page stores only the chosen language in the browser.

## A3. Why we use it, and on what basis
- To provide the app and the booking pages, and to send confirmations, reminders and verification codes: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
- To bill the subscription, which Apple does for us: contract.
- To keep the service secure, prevent abuse and fix problems: our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)).
- To meet legal obligations: Article 6(1)(c).
- For bookings and client information we act as processor: the lawful basis is the professional's. Health information is special category data (Article 9); where it is processed, it is on the basis the professional relies on, normally the client's explicit consent, which the professional collects herself. Bello Hub provides no consent text and asks for no signature: the app only lets her note that a consent exists.
We do not sell personal information and do not share it for advertising.

## A4. Where clinical information lives
Health answers, consent records, notes, photos and recordings live on the professional's iPhone and in her own iCloud, encrypted by the app with a key that is not stored in iCloud. Our servers never hold them in readable form. What a client sends from the booking page travels as a sealed envelope that only the professional's iPhone can open; we keep it until her iPhone has received it and never more than 30 days. If the professional loses both her recovery key and her phone, nobody, including us, can recover this information.

## A5. Who processes data for us (sub-processors)
- Google (Firebase: database, functions, authentication, hosting, push messaging, storage), with servers in the European Union.
- A transactional email provider, for verification codes and booking emails.
- The SMS network Firebase uses to send sign-in codes to professionals who sign in by phone.
- Apple, for Sign in with Apple, the professional's iCloud and App Store billing.
They process data only to provide their service and are bound by data processing terms. We tell professionals in the app before we add or replace one.

## A6. Transfers outside Europe
Our servers are in the European Union. When a provider may access data from another country, we rely on an adequacy decision or on standard contractual clauses and the additional measures they require. Clinical information never reaches our servers in readable form.

## A7. How long we keep it
Bookings leave our servers 7 days after the visit. Sealed envelopes are deleted once received and in any case within 30 days. When a professional deletes a client, the related server data is deleted; when she deletes her account, deletion completes within 30 days and backups age out shortly after. Server logs contain no personal data. The professional decides how long she keeps her own records, subject to the rules of her profession.

## A8. Security
Sensitive fields are encrypted on the device, connections are encrypted, the database refuses direct access, and the page that seals health forms is served from our own domain with a strict content policy. No system is perfectly secure: if our server were compromised, the page that seals the form could in theory be altered. We reduce this risk with the controls described in our security notes, but we do not claim it is impossible.

## A9. Artificial intelligence
Dictation and organising run on the iPhone. What a professional says does not leave the phone. The assistant never diagnoses, and nothing is saved before she reviews it.

## A10. Your rights
You may ask for access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and to object, and you may withdraw a consent at any time without affecting what was done before. A client can use the "Your information" page of her booking or ask the professional, who is the controller of her information; we help the professional answer. Professionals can use the app or write to {{OPERATOR_EMAIL}}. We answer within one month. You may complain to the supervisory authority named in Part B. We never treat you worse for using your rights.

## A11. Children
The service is for professionals and for clients aged 18 or over. A parent or guardian may book for a minor; any consent for a minor's treatment is collected by the professional in person.

## A12. Changes
When we change this policy in a way that matters, we say so in the app and on this page before the change applies, and we keep earlier versions.

# Part B. National annex: United Kingdom (with notes for Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa)

## B1. United Kingdom
The UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 apply. The supervisory authority is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). The United Kingdom recognises the European Union as adequate, so data held in the EU needs no further safeguard; for other countries we use the International Data Transfer Addendum. Professionals who process client information may need to pay the data protection fee to the ICO.

## B2. Ireland
The GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 (Ireland) apply. The supervisory authority is the Data Protection Commission (dataprotection.ie).

## B3. Australia
The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles apply where relevant. Health information is sensitive information. You may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au). Our servers are outside Australia, in the European Union.

## B4. New Zealand
The Privacy Act 2020 applies. You may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (privacy.org.nz).

## B5. South Africa
The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) applies. You may complain to the Information Regulator (inforegulator.org.za).
